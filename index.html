Verbology

Past, Present and Future

Do you know how to express your past, present and future activities?

Audience: teenagers and adults starting English.

Learning outcome: I can talk and write about what I did, what I do and what I am going to do, using verbs in the past, present and future.

You will learn verbs in three times, listen to Leo, read about Maya's weekend, practice with activities and take a final quiz.

Your learning path

Summary: three times, three forms

PAST

Words: yesterday, last week, ago

Form: verb + -ed (or irregular)

I played soccer yesterday.

PRESENT

Words: every day, usually, always

Form: verb (+ -s with he/she/it)

She studies every day.

FUTURE

Words: tomorrow, next week, soon

Form: am/is/are going to + verb, or will + verb

I'm going to cook tomorrow.

Grammar

TimeUseAffirmativeNegative
Past simplefinished actionsI played / I wentI didn't play
Present simpleroutinesShe studiesShe doesn't study
FutureplansI am going to cook / I will call youI'm not going to cook / I won't call

Maya's Weekend

Before reading: Look at the pictures. Which one is the past, the present and the future?

Last Saturday, Maya went to the beach with her family. They swam in the sea and ate fish for lunch. Now it is Sunday and Maya is at home. She usually studies on Sundays, so today she does her homework. Next Saturday, she is going to visit her grandmother and they are going to cook a cake together.

Glossary: beach = sand and sea · swam = past of swim · grandmother = the mother of your mother or father · together = with each other

While and after reading

Compare: What did you do last weekend? Write one sentence: “Last weekend I …”.

1. Recognition: which time is it?

2. Application: choose the correct form

3. Use in context: real situations

Write it!

Write 6 sentences about you: 2 in the past, 2 in the present and 2 in the future.

Criteria: 6 sentences · time words (yesterday, every day, tomorrow) · correct verb form · capital letters and periods

Goal of the site

Verbology helps A1–A2 learners express their past, present and future activities with the right verb forms. Learners listen, read and practice with interactive activities that give feedback, and then check what they learned.

Learning outcome: I can talk and write about what I did, what I do and what I am going to do, using verbs in the past, present and future.
